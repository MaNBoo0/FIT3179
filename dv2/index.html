Chapter 1

How long could my pet live?

These records show the oldest known animals, usually kept in captivity. Most pets live far less, but the records show each species' potential.

Among 23 common pets, lifespan records range from under 4 years for a rat to over half a century for the longest-lived birds. Species matters most.

57 yearsthe longest recorded pet lifespan, a sulphur-crested cockatoo
Birds top the list

The sulphur-crested cockatoo's record is 57 years, the galah's 40.

Cats and dogs sit in the middle

The oldest cat on record reached 30, the oldest dog 27.

Small mammals are shortest

Rats, mice and hamsters have records of about 4 years.

Not only birds

A goldfish's record is 41 years and a snake-necked turtle's 37, longer than any cat or dog.

For owners

Several of the longest-lived pets are native birds: the sulphur-crested cockatoo (57 years), galah (40) and cockatiel (35). A parrot bought today could outlive its owner, so plan who will care for it.

Heavier lives longer

A 20 g mouse's record is 4 years, while a 300 kg horse's is 57.

Birds break the rule

A 30 g budgerigar can reach 21 years, five times as long as a mouse of similar size.

Cats and dogs sit close together

A 4 kg cat's record (30 years) is close to a 40 kg dog's (27), though the dog is ten times heavier.

Dogs break the size rule the other way, as the next chapter shows.

Chapter 2

Which dogs live longest?

Across species, larger animals live longer. Dogs reverse this: small breeds outlive large breeds by about two years. Flat faces shorten lives at every size.

These are breed medians from a UK study of 584,734 dogs, the largest available breed study. A single dog may live much longer or shorter.

Flat faces are shortest at every size

Small long-nosed breeds live longest

13.5 years, the highest of any group.

Size still counts

Every large group sits 1.4 to 2.4 years below its small one.

For owners

If a long life together matters to you, start with a small or medium breed without a flat face.

Chapter 3

Will my pet see its tenth birthday?

Dogs and cats die at similar average ages, but in different patterns. Cats are more likely to die young. Those that survive often outlive dogs by years.

Both studies only include animals that died, so they show the ages pets died at, not how long every pet will live.

Dogs: up to a year longer

Desexed females lived 1.1 years longer than entire ones, and males 0.5 years longer.

Cats: up to two and a half years

Desexed male cats lived 2.5 years longer than entire ones, the biggest gap in the chart.

For owners

Ask your vet about desexing, especially for a male cat. This study shows a link, not proof: desexed pets may also get more care in other ways.

Chapter 4

Which pets are Australians choosing?

Ranks are based on pedigree puppies registered with Dogs Australia, 2018 to 2024, not every dog bought.

Half of Australian homes have a dog, a third a cat. Among pedigree dogs, flat-faced breeds are slipping while the Golden Retriever has reached number one.

Dogs lead

49% of homes have at least one dog, more than any other pet.

Cats come second

34% of homes have a cat. Dogs and cats are the two pets this page follows.

Then fish and birds

Fish (11%) and birds (9%) follow. Small mammals and reptiles are in just 3% of homes each.

Chapter 5

What dangers depend on where I live?

Some dangers depend on location. Paralysis ticks live along Australia's east coast and are most active in spring. Cane toads poison dogs that mouth them. They have spread from Queensland through the Top End into Western Australia.

These Atlas of Living Australia records reflect where people searched, not just where animals live. Most tick records are photos shared since 2015.

Chapter 6

What happens to pets that end up in shelters?

Figures are animals received by RSPCA shelters in each state, from the RSPCA's national statistics. Other shelters and councils are not included.

In 2024–25, RSPCA shelters received 15,852 dogs and 25,639 cats. Many found new homes, but few cats were reclaimed by their owners. Illness-related euthanasia varied widely by state.
